# Independent transit inspection of the reconstructed QLP pilot

Reviewed 2026-10-11 UTC. **None of the three direct period matches or the one harmonic match warrants promotion to a planet candidate on this evidence.** TIC 149392671 remains useful for stellar-variability and source-localization work near 0.477 days. This conclusion concerns the screened signals; it does not establish that these stars have no planets.

The pilot completed 24 targets, 48 sector light curves, and 48 sign-inverted controls. This review read their existing results and visually inspected the individual SAP, KSPSAP, flattened, phase-folded, and event-depth plots for all four transit priorities. It performed no new period search, epoch adjustment, detrending adjustment, clipping, download, or holdout access. Only discovery sectors 1 and 2 are represented. Catalog novelty and source attribution are separate, unresolved questions.

The review is post-screening exploratory interpretation. Both sectors already participated in selection, and the fixed-S1 diagnostics are not a statistically independent validation sample. Reported depths below are local sample-median differences unless explicitly called BLS fitted depths. A positive window is not a detected transit. Conditional BLS depth/error scores quoted here are useful for comparing the original and inverted runs, not as calibrated detection significances.

For TIC 41334745, the matched S1 second peak and S2 first peak both choose the largest trial duration, 0.2 days: approximately 31% of their cycle. The full-phase plots do not show a compelling localized transit. Applying the S1 second-peak ephemeris unchanged to S2 gives 23 ppm flattened depth, 4 ppm without the additional running median, and 71 ppm in SAP. Only half of the 36 measurable flattened events have positive depths; leave-one-event-out mean depths range from −1 to +45 ppm. The S2 inverted light curve has a 0.632754-day, 290-ppm fitted feature with a conditional depth/error score of 5.47, exceeding the original S2 matched feature's 4.59. This is compatible with broad, approximately sign-symmetric fluctuations rather than evidence for repeated occultations. The S1 strongest peak at 2.196423 days also fails to predict S2: its median depth changes from +704 ppm with 9/9 positive S1 windows to −295 ppm with 3/10 positive S2 windows; SAP changes from +702 to −356 ppm. There is no reason here to retain either period as a planet lead.

The diagnostics' geometric-overlap flag for TIC 41334745 should not be mistaken for accurate epoch recovery. Its median independently fitted center separation is 0.1168 days (2.8 hours), with a maximum of 0.2117 days. The permissive threshold is 0.2 days because both boxes are broad. Passing that descriptive flag does not overcome the failed fixed-ephemeris depth behavior.

For TIC 364322406, the S1 first and S2 third peaks have 0.2-day durations, approximately 37% of their 0.544-day cycles. Their median center separation is 0.2254 days, exceeding the 0.2-day geometric-overlap threshold; only 24% of compared centers overlap that broad window. Fixed-S1 S2 depths are −54 ppm after the running median, −101 ppm before it, and −37 ppm in SAP. S2 visibly contains large excursions around BTJD 1365 and 1380, and neighboring negative residuals remain in the corrected products. These are quality concerns rather than an established detector-origin diagnosis. The strongest S2 ordinary BLS peaks at 11.08 and 11.86 days fail the existing event-window triage, whereas inverted S2 peaks near 12.9 days have conditional scores up to 19.26, larger than the original strongest score 10.24. Neither the short-period match nor the sparse long-period peaks supplies a convincing transit sequence.

TIC 149392671 is different: its variability is repeatable enough to merit follow-up, but these BLS peaks look like representations of modulation rather than a planet transit. The independent strongest LS periods are 0.476711 and 0.478290 days, with fitted sinusoidal semi-amplitudes of 283 and 336 ppm. The matched BLS periods are approximately twice those periods and select broad 0.2-day boxes, about 21% of a 0.95-day cycle. The folded curves show broad oscillatory structure, and both sectors' inverted light curves recover approximately 0.95-day peaks. In S1, the inverted 0.955795-day feature has conditional score 7.34, versus 5.66 for the ordinary first peak. The inverted cross-sector direct-period comparison also passes the same screening criteria.

The fixed S1 first-peak ephemeris does retain positive S2 local depth medians: +221 ppm flattened, +224 ppm without the additional median, and +205 ppm in SAP, with 22/27 positive flattened windows and no single event supplying most of the positive-depth sum. That supports further examination of the recurring modulation. It does not distinguish target rotation, pulsation, binary modulation, contamination, or another origin. Also, not every nominally matched BLS pair is phase-consistent: the independent first-to-first peak centers differ by a median 0.4011 days, while first-to-second differs by 0.0843 days. The S1 second-peak ephemeris gives a negative S2 median of −190 ppm. A catalog and pixel-level source audit, comparison stars, and a fixed-frequency modulation model are appropriate next steps; naming a new planet or variable-star class would be premature.

TIC 277099094 supplies the only harmonic-only priority. The S1 third peak at 1.586638 days is close to twice the S2 first period, 0.792389 days. Keeping the S1 epoch, period, and 0.1-day duration fixed produces a negative S2 local median depth of −28 ppm, with 7/15 positive windows; SAP is only +7 ppm. Its S1 first peak near 0.9489 days likewise changes from +48 ppm to −23 ppm when applied to S2. Inverted curves have ordinary-strength peaks, with strongest conditional scores 5.35 and 5.72 compared with 4.57 and 4.96 in the corresponding ordinary first-peak searches. The harmonic period coincidence is insufficient evidence for a transit system.

A useful follow-up clue is that S2 periods near 0.634 days recur in both TIC 41334745 and TIC 277099094: their strongest LS periods are 0.635758 and 0.634734 days, and strongest inverted BLS periods are 0.632754 and 0.633646 days. Similar periods alone do not prove a shared systematic. This warrants comparison of phases, time dependence, and detector locations before assigning either feature to a star.

The existing ranking labels should remain attached to their original exploratory meaning. The combination of at least three positive windows and overlapping broad boxes is permissive, especially at short periods; it is not a planet-vetting criterion. This inspection rejects the current transit interpretation without modifying the frozen screen or retroactively describing the inspection rules as preregistered. The test survey's sensitivity is incomplete, and rejection of these priorities says little about smaller, longer-period, or differently shaped signals outside this search's effective reach.
