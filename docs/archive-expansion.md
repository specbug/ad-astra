# Independent archive expansion

Assessment and retrieval on 2026-10-12 UTC. This is exploratory follow-up after the first TESS QLP pilot; it does not establish a discovery or clear novelty. No protected TESS sector was opened. Public science products, rather than spacecraft engineering telemetry, provide the relevant astronomical measurements.

## Concrete ESA cross-check completed

A fresh ESA Gaia DR3 TAP query of both bright targets and both known RR Lyrae neighbors is saved in `data/archive-expansion/gaia-epoch-availability.json`, with its query, UTC timestamp and SHA-256 sidecar. Both bright targets, Gaia 4757558780144090368 (TIC149392671) and 5476797645662534144 (TIC150104580), have **has_epoch_photometry=false** and **has_epoch_rv=false**. Their catalog NOT_AVAILABLE variability flags do not mean they are constant. Public DR3 epochs therefore cannot test the targets' approximately100–330ppm TESS modulation directly.

Both known RR neighbors have published epoch photometry, which was successfully retrieved from the official [ESA Gaia data server](https://gea.esac.esa.int/data-server/data):

| Neighbor | Exact Gaia DR3 source | Retained G epochs | Baseline | Fixed published period | Descriptive G peak-to-peak fit |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| OGLE-LMC-RRLYR-34351, near TIC149392671 |4757558810204649856|42/45|906.9d|0.4771135961d|1.13mag|
| OGLE-LMC-RRLYR-37553, near TIC150104580 |5476797641361944064|40/42|878.9d|0.5442901668d|0.48mag|

Original VOTables and exact URL/parameters/status/hash provenance are under `data/archive-expansion/gaia-epoch-*`. The files specify **barycentric TCB**, JD origin2455197.5, and contain transit identifiers, uncertainties and rejection/processing flags. The analysis requires exact source identity and this time definition, removes nonfinite/nonpositive measurements and mission-rejected epochs, and preserves remaining processing flags without guessing their meanings. It fits three harmonics at the **already published** periods. See `scripts/archive_expansion_gaia.py`, `runs/archive-expansion/gaia-rr-validation.json`, and the two phase-fold figures in that directory.

The G curves show the expected strong RR variability. Their reduced chi-square under nominal independent epoch errors is55.7 and65.2; BP/RP scatter is worse. Sparse sampling, crowded fields, a low-order template and underestimated/correlated errors limit quantitative templates. The fitted amplitudes are descriptive, not replacements for published catalog measurements. These are independent observations relative to TESS, but the periods and these epochs come from the same Gaia mission. They strengthen the known-contaminant explanation without identifying which object contributes each TESS pixel. No Gaia-TCB versus TESS-TDB absolute phase comparison was attempted.

VizieR's published [OGLE-IV Magellanic Cloud RR Lyrae catalog, J/AcA/66/131](https://vizier.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/VizieR?-source=J/AcA/66/131) was independently queried within90arcsec of both TIC positions. The original XML and SHA sidecars are in `data/archive-expansion/ogle-*`; the identification tables recover the exact two named RR neighbors. This confirms identities/catalog coverage; it is not a newly downloaded OGLE light curve. Direct official OGLE access currently fails at the network proxy.

## MAST coverage metadata completed

Corrected, fully paginated MAST queries searched GALEX, HST, Kepler, K2, SWIFT, XMM, IUE and FUSE. The two first-pilot positions used 0.1-degree cones; the new cohort field used its declared 3-degree radius. Original requests, responses, hash sidecars and completed pagination records are preserved in `data/archive-expansion/mast-v3-*`, with `mast-coverage-summary.json`.

The first target returns 7 GALEX observation/band rows (6 unique observation IDs); 5 have zero distance from the observation footprint, including FUV and NUV, with listed exposures of 105–1648 seconds. The second returns 5 GALEX rows (3 unique IDs); 2 have zero footprint distance in FUV/NUV, with exposures of 172–859 seconds. No other queried collections returned rows at these positions. This is a real UV imaging opportunity, not a UV source detection or verified usable time series. Observation footprints may overlap the search cone without including the star; even zero footprint distance requires image/WCS and calibration review. GALEX resolution is several arcseconds, so the first target’s already known WD companion at 6.2 arcseconds needs a deliberate blend assessment.

The 3-degree second-cohort field returns 260 rows: HST107, SWIFT66, GALEX87, representing 219 unique observation IDs. These are observations somewhere within/intersecting the field, not evidence that the eventual 24 targets have HST or Swift data. Match each selected star to its actual observation footprint before downloading. No Kepler/K2 rows were returned in these queries.

## Which additional archives fit this science?

| Archive / official reference | Useful data | Relevance and limitations here |
|---|---|---|
| [ESA Gaia DR3](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/) |Resolved astrometry, variability, non-single-star solutions, some epoch photometry and RV|Best immediate contaminant identification. Scan-law sampling is irregular; published epoch products are a selected subset. G/BP/RP passbands differ from TESS. Existing bright-target DR3 epochs are absent, and a source lacking a published variable classification is not cleared as constant.|
| [CHEOPS archive / guest-observer programme](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/cheops-guest-observers-programme/archive) |Targeted broad optical transit time series|Potentially excellent validation or transit-parameter refinement **if the specific star was observed and data are public**. It is not an all-sky survey. Exposure/cadence, aperture blending, orbit-related gaps and visit baseline must be checked per product. No coverage for these targets is established in this assessment.|
| [CoRoT](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/corot) |Optical transit/asteroseismology light curves in selected fields|Valuable for a separate archive-selected search. The high-southern-latitude fields here are not the familiar Galactic-plane CoRoT exoplanet fields; no target coverage is established. Avoid treating its archive as all-sky.|
| [Kepler at MAST](https://archive.stsci.edu/missions-and-data/kepler), [K2](https://archive.stsci.edu/missions-and-data/k2) |High-precision optical light curves, pixel products, published catalogs|Strong choice for a separately declared future cohort, especially reanalysis of timing or blending in known systems. Original Kepler surveyed a fixed northern field and K2 observed near the ecliptic; the present southern, high-ecliptic-latitude cohorts are poor overlap choices. Product-specific cadence, detrending and crowded apertures still matter.|
| [ASAS-SN](https://asas-sn.osu.edu/) |Long-baseline ground-based optical monitoring|All-sky nature makes it worth a **large-amplitude/activity/eclipse** check. Typical ground precision, large pixels and daily/seasonal aliases cannot validate a100ppm TESS signal. The G~19 RR neighbors are much fainter than the bright targets and not automatically usable in ASAS-SN. No target forced-photometry retrieval was completed here.|
| [OGLE](https://ogle.astrouw.edu.pl/) |Dense I/V photometry in selected crowded southern fields|Best independent ground-based RR-neighbor template if exact published light curves are accessible. The named neighbors are cataloged OGLE objects, so identity coverage is real. Bright TIC primaries need saturation/crowding assessment. The newRA7.199/Dec−59.954 field is not assumed covered. Daily aliases and filter/epoch differences require explicit handling.|
| [ZTF at IRSA](https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/Missions/ztf.html) |Repeated g/r/i ground-based imaging and catalogs|Good for a new northern transient/variable cohort, but the current Dec−60° to−64° targets lie outside its usual northern survey footprint. Do not spend effort expecting precise optical overlap here.|
| [ISRO AstroSat archive](https://astrobrowse.issdc.gov.in/astro_archive/archive/Home.jsp) |Targeted UVIT ultraviolet images/photometry; X-ray instrument observations|UV resolution may distinguish a hot WD companion or activity-related source if an actual matching observation exists. It is not a broad-band optical transit survey: UV/X-ray variability and a100ppm optical transit are different observables. Targeted pointing coverage, detector/filter calibration, event-time corrections, source confusion and exposure must precede interpretation. No AstroSat observation of these fields was verified.|
| [GALEX at MAST](https://archive.stsci.edu/missions-and-data/galex) |UV source catalog/images and selected time-resolved exposures|A useful spectral-energy-distribution check for the **already known WD companion** of TIC149392671, Actual overlapping FUV/NUV footprint metadata has now been found; image/WCS, resolution and saturation still need checking. This would characterize a known system. A UV catalog match alone is not time-series validation or a new companion.|
| [ESA PLATO](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Plato) |Future long-baseline precision photometry|Do not assume public science time series exist today. Current mission/archive availability was not verified because the official host is blocked. It is a future planning option, not evidence in this study.|

Instrument capabilities above are broad suitability assessments, not verified per-target precision or detections. Archive access and science coverage are separate questions. A host blocked by this environment says nothing about whether observations exist.

## Recommended next work

1. Use the completed Gaia G curves, then obtain the exact **OGLE RR-neighbor photometry** if access becomes available. Verify the OGLE time convention and quality conventions before a period/phase comparison. Compare independently observed RR morphology, rather than expecting these faint, crowded sources to supply a precise TESS-band contamination fraction.
2. For any convincing second-pilot transit lead, make a target-specific CHEOPS/ground-observatory coverage query, then check public products and precision before retrieving them. For a genuinely broader search, a **separate Kepler/K2 or ground-variable cohort** should be selected from archive coverage with declared discovery/validation data, control injections and catalog/literature novelty checks. Adding unmatched missions to the current stars does not create independent validation.
