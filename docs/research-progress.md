# Research progress — 12 October 2026

No new planet, previously uncataloged star, novel stellar system, or justified catalog-parameter correction has been established. **TIC 201308024 is retained as an unresolved approximately 4.19-day variability follow-up lead**, supported by alternative image extraction and concerning independent Gaia astrometric/RV diagnostics; its physical interpretation and novelty remain unconfirmed. Two reconstructed exploratory TESS pilots have completed: **48 distinct stars, 96 sector-1/2 science light curves and 96 matched sign-reversed analyses**, with no failed science products. These counts exclude the earlier lost 88-star work. Scores, catalog nonmatches and repeated period matches are not discovery evidence on their own.

## First sample

The [first-pilot report](research-report.md) contains the completed 24-star experiment, controls, transit rejections and variability follow-up. Its approximately 0.477-day aperture signal closely matches a known RR Lyrae 29.25 arcseconds from TIC 149392671. Sector-1/2 pixel analysis remains inconclusive about attribution. TIC 150104580 has weaker approximately 1.2-day structure, unstable phase and a different known RR neighbor; the near daily-alias arithmetic is not a demonstrated explanation. Neither target has been promoted as a newly variable star.

## Second sample selected before inspecting flux

A separate 3-degree field centered at ICRS RA 7.1992341074, Dec −59.9537750245 was fixed from TESS pointing geometry, away from the first sample. Its complete TIC query returned 448 rows; dated NASA/ExoFOP, history and literature exclusions left 447. Fixed seed 20261011 selected the first 24 eligible targets among 40 examined ranks. Three MAST HTTP503 failures remained recorded selection exclusions. This availability bias and the conservative catalog cuts limit population interpretation.

The prior replacement field at RA 0, Dec −80 lacked sector 2 in all 348 completed metadata states. Its declaration, incomplete selection, failures and coverage-stop rationale remain preserved. No flux was used to choose either field. The successful field was a separate declared metadata experiment.

In addition to target-centered SIMBAD checks, the second sample excluded any known Gaia DR3 `VARIABLE` source within 90 arcseconds, with independent cone counts. Unknown flags and multiple nearby Gaia sources remained allowed and recorded. This does not make the stars constant or the apertures free of blends. [Further catalog review](qlp-overlapfield-prior.md) returned all 72 ASAS-SN/VSX/GCVS tables with no rows within 10 arcseconds; Gaia's 27 positional sources had no published variability classification or rotation period. Catalog coverage is scoped, and coordinates were not propagated between epochs.

`data/qlp-overlapfield-frozen/{manifest,config}.json` binds the metadata, ordering, product identities, unchanged screening source and existing injection controls. All 48 science curves and 48 sign controls completed in `runs/qlp-overlapfield-pilot/results.json`; sectors 68/69 remain unopened. This sample is disjoint from the reconstructed first 24; overlap with the historical lost 88 remains unknown. Only literal sectors 1/2 were retrieved for science.

The four new box-search priorities were TIC 425933704 (direct period agreement) and TIC 231910457, 234490600 and 257655479 (factor-two/three harmonics). [Independent vetting](qlp-overlapfield-vetting.md) rejected promotion: matching periods failed phase or lacked persuasive fixed-ephemeris repetition. The direct pair's predicted centers differ by approximately 0.171 days, exceeding the approximately 0.052-day duration-based tolerance, with zero model-window overlap. None of the 24 had strongest corrected-flux Lomb–Scargle period agreement within the declared 1% rule; this is not an absence-of-variability result.

All 24 diagnostic dossiers and eight contact sheets were inspected, including less-processed SAP measurements. TIC 201308024, 355767420 and 281593180 show substantial SAP structure suppressed in the corrected data. The [postselection SAP investigation](qlp-overlapfield-sap.md) completed 144 comparison fits and leave-target-out detector-median regressions. Those regressions preserve much of the variation, but can miss position-dependent aperture losses and unknown variable neighbors. The latter two targets peak at the 13-day search boundary, so no resolved period is established.

For TIC 201308024, fixing the S1 2.032702-day period gives SAP amplitudes 1,853 and 356 ppm, with a roughly 0.410-cycle phase change. A separately declared descriptive S2 search instead peaks at 4.194743 days, approximately twice the S1 period with a 3.2% difference from an exact harmonic. Evolving rotational spot patterns can change phase and harmonic dominance; the fixed-frequency failure alone does not reject stellar rotation. These searches are postselection and provide no calibrated significance or novelty claim.

Separately declared [7×7 and 11×11 original-image reductions](qlp-sap-pixel-201308024.md) checked the fixed periods, robust regression, multiple apertures and background models. A G=9.70 neighbor, TIC 201308023, lies 92.29 arcseconds away and was just outside the preselection's 90-arcsecond filter. Its wings dominate much of the S1 signal. The larger S2 extraction retains roughly **1,754 ppm** target-aperture modulation at 4.194743 days after a joint neighbor/background/centroid model. The smaller cutout's apparently unique target pixel maximum was frame-dependent: the expanded frame also reveals modulation around the bright neighbor. Aperture losses, unresolved sources and correlated pixel errors prevent a confident intrinsic-source attribution. No physical period uncertainty or calibrated detection significance is assigned.

[Independent stellar metadata](qlp-sap-stellar-201308024.md) adds concrete reasons for follow-up: primary Gaia RUWE is approximately **29.6**, its RV constancy p-value is represented as zero, and its robust RV amplitude is approximately **60.6 km/s** over 964 days. These are public summary statistics, not epoch velocities or an orbit; crowded-source/pipeline problems remain possible. No Gaia two-body orbit is reported. Flagged GALAH broadening near 16.6 km/s lies near the conditional equatorial speed for a 4.19-day rotation and the TIC radius, but includes nonrotational broadening and cannot establish rotation. The bright neighbor has an already published Gaia spectroscopic trend without an orbital period. Neither a newly discovered binary nor a common-distance system has been established.

## Independent data and literature

The [archive assessment](archive-expansion.md) includes an actual ESA Gaia cross-check, rather than only a list of missions. Both named RR neighbors have downloaded DR3 epoch photometry: 42 and 40 retained G epochs over roughly 907 and 879 days. Fixed published-period harmonic folds show strong known RR variation. Nominal-error reduced chi-square remains large, so these are descriptive curves, not precise contamination templates. The two bright targets lack published Gaia DR3 epoch photometry; no TCB/TDB absolute phase comparison was performed.

MAST returned GALEX FUV/NUV footprint overlap for the first-pilot targets, and HST/Swift/GALEX observations intersecting the second field. Those field observations have not been matched individually to all selected stars or downloaded as science products. No Kepler/K2 overlap was returned. CHEOPS, CoRoT, AstroSat, ASAS-SN, OGLE and ZTF suitability is assessed with pointing, cadence, precision and passband limits. Several official hosts were proxy-blocked; network-domain additions were saved in the environment draft, without claiming runtime application or publication.

[ADS checks](ads-literature-review.md) authenticated successfully using a transient credential. Public responses and hashes are saved; the credential is absent from research files. Initial broad alias searches produced unrelated weather/theory papers. More specific astronomy-only TIC/Tycho/OGLE queries had no matches, as did scoped TIC/2MASS searches for the three SAP leads. Known RR sources also yield identifier nonmatches, demonstrating why ADS full-text indexing cannot provide novelty clearance. The known Raddi white-dwarf-pair paper was retrieved directly by bibcode.

A further ADS topic search returned the newest 100 of 145 TESS-rotation records and is explicitly recorded as **truncated**. [Targeted published rotation checks](rotation-prior-review.md) found approximately 2.37-day TIC 281593180 and 1.2-day TIC 150104580 peaks already in released TARS sector-1/2 headers, without claiming those peaks are confirmed primary rotation periods. TIC 149392671 is in Claytor's rotation-nondetection sample. TIC 201308024 has no indexed TARS S1/S2 product by exact TIC or a 5-arcsecond positional query; its exact Gaia DR2 parallax of 1.676 mas fails TARS's stated DR2 >2 mas selection. Absence is therefore not evidence of novelty. The million-star aggregate catalog remains unchecked because CDS has no available table and the advertised Zenodo host was blocked. A new domain addition was saved for review; runtime access and exhaustive prior-art clearance are not established.

## Third round: completed independent checks

Network access subsequently allowed the [full TARS adopted-period catalogue review](tars-aggregate-review.md). All **1,046,317 rows** were scanned, with publisher MD5, length and ZIP integrity verified. TIC 201308024 and bright neighbor TIC 201308023 have no exact TIC or exact Gaia-release identifier match. This confirms scoped absence from the default adopted-period catalogue, not novelty or absence from the much larger parent measurement table. TIC 281593180 does have an adopted **17.1732 ± 1.1848-day** period, with multiple-period and doubled-period flags; that published result is not our discovery. Its contributing later-sector measurements were read as catalogue metadata only, without opening later-sector flux.

[Independent ASAS-SN V-band observations](ground-validation-201308024.md) now provide **258 measurements each** for the primary and bright neighbor across about **1,594 days**. Exact positional identities, full CSVs, original pages and timing/quality limits are retained. Target errors are typically 0.020 mag, much larger than the roughly 0.002-mag signal of interest. A fixed 4.194743-day fit gives 0.002098-mag amplitude with approximately 0.001741-mag conditional white-noise amplitude error: this is **inconclusive**, not independent confirmation. Bright-neighbor scatter substantially exceeds its quoted errors; daily and seasonal sampling aliases are strong. Field-center HJD has an estimated 200-second timing accuracy and no documented timescale, so no absolute BJD-TDB phase comparison was made. Injection-response checks verify fitting response rather than completeness or significance.

[Scoped binary prior-art queries](binary-prior-201308024.md) found no exact target identity in the queried GALAH binary catalogue and no SB9 row within 10 arcseconds. These limited catalogues do not provide binary novelty clearance. TIC 201308024 remains an unresolved variability follow-up lead: the new data neither establish its physical interpretation nor rule it out.

## Third round: stronger source-association checks

Complete [alternate-product metadata](alternate-tess-201308024.md) identified several additional S1/S2 reductions of the primary and native 120-second SPOC products of its bright neighbor. The primary is inside both actual native target-pixel stamps; the small target apertures are complete and separate from the bright-neighbor aperture. Narrow-cone query failures remain recorded and were not treated as absence.

The [native SPOC analysis](native-spoc-201308023.md) retains approximately **2,447 ppm** S2 modulation at the fixed 4.194743-day period in a robust 3×3 primary-aperture fit with neighbor, background and pointing controls. Removing each observed day in turn gives **2,373–2,545 ppm**, with fitted phase changes under 0.010 cycles. Neighbor native SAP/PDCSAP fits are about **13 ppm** at that period. All 232 robust/day-deletion fits converge with original input hashes unchanged. This weakens a few outlying samples, a single day or straightforward bright-neighbor flux modulation as complete explanations; position-dependent losses and unresolved sources remain possible.

[Gaia-informed TGLC forward-model photometry](tglc-201308024.md) independently reduces the same full-frame images using a neighboring-source PSF model. S2 raw PSF and decontaminated-aperture robust amplitudes are approximately **3,168 and 3,100 ppm**. S1 raw 2.032702-day modulation also persists, while its 4.19-day fits are more sensitive to robust weighting. Producer-calibrated curves already underwent 1-day detrending, attenuating multiday signals; their smaller amplitudes do not invalidate the raw results. These are complementary reductions/cadences of TESS, not independent telescope confirmation or evidence for one coherent physical clock across both sectors.

A [fixed-frequency field comparison](sap-field-frequency-201308024.md) places the primary first among all 24 QLP SAP stars in both sectors. S2 robust amplitude is approximately **2,505 ppm**, versus **264 ppm** for the next largest star on the same detector. This strengthens source association relative to a uniformly shared instrumental trend, without excluding position-dependent systematics or providing a null-survey significance.

The resulting status is **a stronger target-associated variability follow-up candidate**, with independent ground confirmation still inconclusive and rotation/binarity/novelty unestablished. It is not a new uncataloged star. No later-season flux has been opened.
