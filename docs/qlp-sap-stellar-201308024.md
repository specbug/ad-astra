The naive inverse parallaxes are approximately 323, 89 and 1005 pc, respectively; these are descriptive inversions rather than inferred distances. The extremely high primary RUWE makes its single-star astrometry problematic. The primary has 22 Gaia RV transits, RV -8.53 ± 4.12 km/s and vbroad 22.56 ± 15.61 km/s. RV variability and high RUWE motivate a binary or other astrometric/spectroscopic explanation, but do not classify a system. No duplicated-source flag is set, and all three lack public Gaia epoch photometry and have phot_variable_flag NOT_AVAILABLE. Neither flag means constant flux. The bright neighbor is SIMBAD CD-60 23, exactly TIC 201308023, and is already marked by Gaia non_single_star=2. The strong parallax differences do not support treating these three objects as a simple common-distance system; proper-motion/astrometric modelling and published system literature would be required.

The primary's exact Gaia DR2 parallax is **1.67589 ± 0.21422 mas**. The preserved official TARS ReadMe states selection requires **Gaia DR2 parallax >2 mas**, not DR3 parallax or a TIC distance estimate. This primary fails that stated criterion even though its DR3 parallax exceeds 2 mas and the legacy TIC distance is approximately 381 pc. This provides a concrete selection explanation consistent with its absence from indexed TARS products; it does not independently establish aggregate-catalogue nonmembership or explain the DR2/DR3 astrometric change.

Both requested GALAH tables were returned with one exact-source row. DR3 reports Vbroad 16.586 ± 2.331 km/s, Flagsp=9 and redflag=1; the DR3 stellar-quality bitmask combines bit 1 (Gaia RUWE >1.4) and bit 8 (reduction issues), so this is flagged spectroscopy. DR4 reports fitted broadening `vsini` 16.677 ± 1.483 km/s, flagSp=0, flagSpFit=0, and flagRed=2048. The DR4 field description explicitly says this is fitted sme.vsini with sme.vmac=0: it includes broadening that cannot be assigned uniquely to rotation. The preserved ReadMe's G1 bitmask describes **flagSp**, not flagRed; its numeric 2048 definition must not be transferred to the reduction flag. The reduction flag's physical meaning remains unchecked here.

Using TIC radius 1.3017 solar radii and assuming rotation would imply equatorial speeds approximately 32.4 km/s at 2.0327 d or 15.7 km/s at 4.19474 d. These assumptions are unverified: SAP periods change between sectors, source localization is unresolved, spectroscopy is flagged, and line broadening is not a clean projected rotation measurement. No inclination, intrinsic rotation period, novel binary or new variable-star claim follows from this comparison.
