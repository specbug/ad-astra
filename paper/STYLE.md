# Writing standard for the TIC 402710280.01 manuscript

Calibrated against Nature Astronomy research articles published in October 2026 (Williams et al., second-generation
planet candidate around a white dwarf; Turrini et al., two families of multi-planet systems; Alderson et al., limb map
of WASP-17 b), and applied within the AAS journal conventions (American spelling, serial comma, AASTeX).

## Sentences

1. One claim per sentence. The subject is a physical thing (the star, the transit, the offset) or "we". Data,
   periods and models do not act: a period does not "predict" and a shape is not "disfavored for" a pair.
2. Results come with their comparison value: "1.5″ ± 1.3″ from the target", "0.7σ", "compared with 29.2 min".
3. A hedge is one calibrated word ("consistent with", "indicate", "candidate"), placed once, where it applies.
4. At most one semicolon per paragraph, and only between two closely related claims, never as a list separator in prose.
5. No stacked noun phrases, no "which" chains, no "in order to", no "it is worth noting", no "notably".
6. "Therefore" and "so" only where an inference follows from the sentence before it.
7. Terms keep one meaning throughout: a *transit* is an observed event; an *epoch* is a cycle number; a *window* is
   the ±0.8 d data segment around a transit.

## Paragraphs

1. The first sentence states the paragraph's point; the rest supports it.
2. Methods are told in the order they were done, in the past tense for what we did and the present for what is true.
3. Every number in the abstract appears, with its derivation, in the body.

## Detail

1. Every quantitative claim traces to a results file, a table, or a stated calculation; every citation resolves to the
   cited work (DOI or ASCL record checked).
2. Numbers that recur (period, depth, rms, offsets) are identical everywhere, including the pre-registration.
3. Figures use the text's spelling and terms, embed TrueType fonts, and have no overlapping labels.
