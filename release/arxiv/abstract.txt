We report a transiting sub-Neptune candidate with a period of 36.34 days around TIC 402710280 (TYC 9041-03677-1), a K dwarf of TESS magnitude 9.24 at 66.8 pc that the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) has observed only in full-frame images. Four transits, recorded between 2021 and 2026 in Sectors 39, 93, 100 and 102 and reduced by three independent pipelines, share a depth of $2092 \pm 47$ ppm and a duration of $5.27 \pm 0.05$ h. Two of them fall on consecutive cycles, and every shorter period predicts transits in covered data that are absent at $\geq 18.6\sigma$; the period is $P = 36.33986 \pm 0.00051$ d. A transit model gives $R_p/R_\star = 0.0421^{+0.0007}_{-0.0005}$ and a stellar density consistent with the host's. The candidate has a radius of $3.75 \pm 0.22 R_\oplus$ and an equilibrium temperature of $498 \pm 21$ K. Difference-image centroids place the signal $1.5'' \pm 1.3''$ from the target. The only catalogued star close enough to contribute, a background star $3.5''$ away, would have to host an eclipsing binary whose eclipse shape is disfavoured for any main-sequence pair ($\Delta\chi^2 \geq 142$). No secondary eclipse is detected, and TRICERATOPS gives a false-positive probability of $0.003 \pm 0.004$. No high-resolution imaging exists, so companions within about $1''$ are constrained only by Gaia astrometry. The mid-times scatter about a linear ephemeris by 21.8 min (rms). Quick-Look Pipeline light curves of the 2026 sectors reproduce the largest excess, which therefore does not arise in our photometry; we report the scatter without choosing between an instrumental and a dynamical origin. The light curve also contains a $407 \pm 26$ ppm signal with a period of 3.78 days whose source, the target or the background star, the present data cannot identify. We give predicted transit times, including a transit in TESS Sector 114 on 2027 February 19.
