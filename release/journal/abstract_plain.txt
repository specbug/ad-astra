Transiting sub-Neptunes on orbits longer than a month are rare around bright stars. We report a candidate with a 36.34-day period around TIC 402710280, a V = 10.0 K dwarf at 66.8 pc that the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) has observed only in full-frame images. Four transits from 2021 to 2026, reduced by three independent pipelines, have consistent depths (mean 2092 ± 47 ppm) and a duration of 5.27 ± 0.05 h. Two are 36.36 days apart, and the light curves exclude every shorter period consistent with both, which fixes the period at P = 36.33986 ± 0.00051 d. The candidate's radius is 3.75 ± 0.22 R⊕ and its equilibrium temperature 498 ± 21 K. Difference-image centroids place the signal 1.5″ ± 1.3″ from the target, and eclipse models of the only nearby Gaia source, 3.5″ away, fit worse than a planet by Δχ² ≥142 for every main-sequence pair. TRICERATOPS gives a false-positive probability of 0.003 ± 0.004; without high-resolution imaging, the object remains a candidate. The transit times scatter about a linear ephemeris by 21.8 min (rms), and an independent reduction reproduces the largest departure. Four transits cannot separate timing systematics from transit-timing variations. A second signal, of 407 ± 26 ppm at 3.78 days, may come from the target, an unresolved companion, or the background star. We predict a transit in TESS Sector 114 on 2027 February 19.
