We report a transiting sub-Neptune candidate with a period of 36.34 days around TIC 402710280, a K dwarf of TESS magnitude 9.24 at 66.8 pc that the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) has observed only in full-frame images. Four transits in Sectors 39, 93, 100, and 102 (2021–2026), reduced by three independent pipelines, have consistent depths (mean 2092 ± 47 ppm) and a duration of 5.27 ± 0.05 h. Two fall on consecutive cycles, and every shorter period predicts transits absent from covered data at ≥18.6σ; P = 36.33986 ± 0.00051 d. The candidate has a radius of 3.75 ± 0.22 R⊕ and an equilibrium temperature of 498 ± 21 K. Difference-image centroids place the signal 1.5″ ± 1.3″ from the target. The only cataloged star close enough to contribute, 3.5″ away, would need an eclipse whose shape is disfavored for any main-sequence pair (Δχ² ≥142). No secondary eclipse is detected, and TRICERATOPS gives a false-positive probability of 0.003 ± 0.004; without high-resolution imaging we do not claim validation. The mid-times scatter about a linear ephemeris by 21.8 min (rms). Quick-Look Pipeline light curves reproduce the 29-min excess in the interval between the 2026 transits, so it is not an artifact of our photometry; its origin, instrumental or dynamical, remains open. A 407 ± 26 ppm signal with a period of 3.78 days comes from either the target or the background star. We predict transit times, including one in TESS Sector 114 on 2027 February 19.
