Dear Editor,

I submit "A 36.34-day Sub-Neptune Candidate Transiting the Nearby K Dwarf TIC 402710280" for consideration in the
Astronomical Journal, in the corridor The Solar System, Exoplanets, and Astrobiology.

The paper reports a new transiting sub-Neptune candidate (3.75 Earth radii, equilibrium temperature 500 K) around a
V = 10.0 K dwarf at 67 pc. TESS has observed the star only in full-frame images, and it is not a TOI or a community TOI.
Four transits between 2021 and 2026 have consistent depths in three independent reductions; two fall on consecutive
cycles, which fixes the period at 36.34 days. Difference-image centroids place the signal 1.5" +/- 1.3" from the target,
eclipses of the one nearby Gaia source are disfavored for any main-sequence pair, and TRICERATOPS gives a false-positive
probability of 0.003; without high-resolution imaging I classify the object as a candidate.

The transit times scatter about a linear ephemeris by 22 minutes. The paper tests the instrumental explanations it can
and reports the scatter without choosing between instrumental and dynamical origins. While checking it, I found that the
QLP light curves of Sectors 100 and 102 carry a single barycentric correction per sector, an offset of about two minutes;
I have reported this to the QLP team. The next transit, on 2027 February 19, falls in TESS Sector 114; the test of that
prediction is pre-registered on Zenodo (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23390325).

The preprint is on arXiv (arXiv:2610.26056) and Zenodo (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23390316); the data and code that reproduce every
number are archived at doi:10.5281/zenodo.23390273. The use of a large language model in preparing the work is
disclosed in the acknowledgments. The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, and I have no competing interests.

I am an independent researcher without grant funding and request support from the AAS Publication Support Fund.
