Dear Editor,

I submit "A 36.34-day Sub-Neptune Candidate Transiting the Nearby K Dwarf TIC 402710280" for consideration in the
Astronomical Journal, in the corridor The Solar System, Exoplanets, and Astrobiology.

The paper reports a new transiting sub-Neptune candidate, with a radius of 3.75 Earth radii and an equilibrium
temperature of 500 K, around a V = 10.0 K dwarf at 67 pc. TESS has observed the star only in full-frame images, and it
is neither a TOI nor a community TOI. Four transits recorded between 2021 and 2026 have consistent depths in three
independent reductions. Two are 36.36 days apart. The light curves exclude every shorter period consistent with both
down to one day, and the duration disfavors still shorter ones, leaving a period of 36.34 days. Difference-image
centroids place the signal 1.5" +/- 1.3" from the target, eclipse models of the one nearby Gaia source fit worse than
the planet model for every main-sequence pair, and TRICERATOPS gives a false-positive probability of 0.003. Without
high-resolution imaging, I classify the object as a candidate.

The transit times scatter about a linear ephemeris by 22 minutes. The paper tests the instrumental explanations it can
and reports the scatter without choosing between an instrumental and a dynamical origin. In checking the timing, I found
that the QLP time stamps of Sectors 100 and 102 appear to use a single barycentric correction for each sector, which
offsets them by about two minutes at the transits; I have reported this to the QLP team. A transit is predicted in
TESS Sector 114 on 2027 February 19, and the test of that prediction is pre-registered on Zenodo
(doi:10.5281/zenodo.23390325).

The preprint is on arXiv (arXiv:2610.26056), and the data and code that reproduce the results are archived on Zenodo
(doi:10.5281/zenodo.23553448). The acknowledgments disclose the use of a large language model in preparing the work.
The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, and I have no competing interests.
