\section{DDT Justification}

TIC~402710280 (TYC~9041-03677-1, $T = 9.24$) hosts a transiting sub-Neptune candidate, TIC~402710280.01, with $P = 36.33986 \pm 0.00051$~d \citep{Vora2026}. \tess\ has observed the star only in full-frame images (FFIs). The period could be fixed only once the Sector~100 and 102 transits (2026 February and March) fell on consecutive cycles, so the target was not proposed in the Cycle~9 Guest Investigator call. \tess\ will observe the star in Sectors~113 and~114 (camera~4), and according to \texttt{tess-point} \citep{Burke2020} it will not observe it again through Sector~134 (2028 September). The transit predicted for 2027 February~19 in Sector~114 is therefore the only transit \tess\ can record for at least the next two years. Without a DDT allocation it will be recorded only in 200-s FFIs, and no target pixel files or SPOC two-minute light curves will exist.

\section{Scientific Justification}

Four transits, in Sectors~39, 93, 100 and~102, share a depth of $2092 \pm 47$~ppm and a duration of $5.27 \pm 0.05$~h (Figure~\ref{fig:transits}). They come from three independent reductions (TESS-SPOC, QLP and our own FFI photometry). Every shorter alias period predicts transits in covered data that are absent at $\geq 18.6\sigma$. With the host's TIC parameters ($T_{\rm eff} = 5182$~K, $R_\star = 0.82$\,\rsun, $M_\star = 0.88$\,\msun) the candidate has $R_p = 3.75 \pm 0.22$\,\re, $a = 0.206$~AU and $T_{\rm eq} \approx 500$~K. The vetting is as follows:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item Difference-image centroids place the signal $1.5'' \pm 1.3''$ from the target.
\item The only \textit{Gaia} star close enough to contribute ($3.5''$ away, 354 times fainter) would need a 74\% eclipse, whose shape is disfavoured for any main-sequence pair ($\Delta\chi^2 \geq 142$).
\item No secondary eclipse is detected.
\item \texttt{TRICERATOPS} \citep{Giacalone2021} gives ${\rm FPP} = 0.003 \pm 0.004$ and ${\rm NFPP} < 10^{-6}$. No high-resolution imaging exists, so we treat these as upper bounds on the evidence rather than a validation.
\end{itemize}
Long-period, temperate sub-Neptunes around stars this bright are uncommon among known planets. The expected radial-velocity semi-amplitude, about 2.8~\ms\ for a mass of about 14~$M_\oplus$, is within reach of current spectrographs.

Two-minute data in Sector~114 would address three open questions.

\textbf{(1) The registered test of the ephemeris.} The transit times scatter about a linear ephemeris by 21.8~min rms, against formal uncertainties of 1.7--3.3~min. We find no instrumental cause, and four epochs cannot separate timing systematics from transit-timing variations. We registered the prediction for epoch~57 (BJD$_{\rm TDB}$~2461455.978~$\pm$~30~min) and the rules that decide its outcome before the data exist \citep{PreReg}. That protocol names SPOC two-minute light curves as the first-choice data, ahead of any FFI product. Its pixel-level check of the signal's position uses target pixel files.

\textbf{(2) An independent measurement of the timing.} Our reductions produced two of the four mid-times. A SPOC light curve gives a mid-time from photometry that we did not make. If the timing scatter comes from our light-curve production, it should disappear in SPOC data. If it comes from a perturbing body, it should persist.

\textbf{(3) The 3.78-day signal.} The light curve also contains a $407 \pm 26$~ppm, 1.3-h signal with $P = 3.78371$~d \citep{Vora2026}. It may come from the target, possibly as a grazing transit, or from the $3.5''$ neighbour. Sectors~113 and~114 together contain about a dozen of its events. At two-minute cadence they would resolve its ingress shape, which tests the grazing geometry. The target pixel files would also tighten its difference-image centroid, which is currently $5.2'' \pm 6.7''$ from the target.

\section{Target Justification and Feasibility}

The request is for one target, TIC~402710280 (RA~$248.5236^\circ$, Dec~$-63.5497^\circ$), at two-minute cadence in Sectors~113 and~114. Sector~114 has priority 1.0. \texttt{tess-point} places the star on silicon in both sectors: camera~4, CCD~4 (column~385, row~209) in Sector~113, and camera~4, CCD~1 (column~646, row~475) in Sector~114. The TIC contamination ratio is 0.06, and there are no stars brighter than 7th magnitude in the field.

The epoch-57 transit is predicted for 2027 February~19 at 11:29~UTC, with $\sigma = 30$~min (34~min including the covariance of $T_0$ and $P$). This is about six days after the sector begins, well inside the first orbit. The adjacent transits (2027 January~14 and March~27) fall outside the two sectors.

In FFIs each transit is measured with a depth uncertainty of 86--144~ppm, so a single transit is detected at about 15--24$\sigma$. SPOC photometry at $T = 9.2$ should do at least as well. Ingress and egress each last about 14~min, so they span seven two-minute cadences compared with four 200-s cadences. This improves the mid-time and the shape constraint, which is the discriminant between the planet and a diluted eclipse of the neighbour.
