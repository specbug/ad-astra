\section{DDT Justification}

TIC~402710280 (TYC~9041-03677-1; $T = 9.24$, $V = 9.98$) hosts a transiting sub-Neptune candidate with $P = 36.33986 \pm 0.00051$~d, designated TIC~402710280.01 in \citet{Vora2026}. \tess\ has observed the star only in full-frame images (FFIs), and no TOI, CTOI or SPOC threshold-crossing event exists for it. The period was fixed only by the transit of 2026 March~29 in Sector~102, consecutive with that of Sector~100 and after the Cycle~9 Guest Investigator deadline of 2026 March~10. It is on none of the Cycle~9 two-minute or 20-s target lists for Sectors~113 and~114 (ExoFOP, MAST and the target lists checked 2026 October~18). According to \texttt{tess-point} 0.9.5 \citep{Burke2020}, with provisional pointings, \tess\ will observe it in those two sectors and not again through Sector~134 (2028 September). The transit of 2027 February~19, in Sector~114, is therefore the only one \tess\ will record before late 2028. Without a DDT allocation it will be recorded in 200-s FFIs only, with no target pixel files and no SPOC light curve.

\section{Scientific Justification}

Four transits (Sectors~39, 93, 100 and~102; Figure~\ref{fig:transits}), measured in three independent reductions (TESS-SPOC, QLP and our own FFI photometry), have consistent depths ($\chi^2 = 2.5$ for 3 degrees of freedom; weighted mean $2092 \pm 47$~ppm) and a duration of $5.27 \pm 0.05$~h. Every shorter alias predicts transits in covered data that are absent: $P/2$ in Sectors~39, 101 and~102 at $\geq 28.5\sigma$, and $P/3$ to $P/5$ at $\geq 18.6\sigma$. For the TIC stellar parameters ($T_{\rm eff} = 5182$~K, $R_\star = 0.82$\,\rsun, $M_\star = 0.88$\,\msun), the candidate has $R_p = 3.75 \pm 0.22$\,\re, $a = 0.206$~AU and $T_{\rm eq} \approx 500$~K. Difference-image centroids place the signal $1.5'' \pm 1.3''$ from the target. The only \textit{Gaia} source near enough to contribute, $3.5''$ away and 354 times fainter, would need a 74\% eclipse, whose shape is disfavoured for any main-sequence pair ($\Delta\chi^2 \geq 142$). No secondary eclipse is detected. \texttt{TRICERATOPS} \citep{Giacalone2021} gives ${\rm FPP} = 0.003 \pm 0.004$ and ${\rm NFPP} < 10^{-6}$; without high-resolution imaging we treat these as supporting evidence, not as validation. A mass of about 14~$M_\oplus$ would give a radial-velocity semi-amplitude of about 2.8~\ms.

Two-minute data would serve three purposes. The 36-day transit falls in Sector~114 only; Sector~113 supplies the out-of-transit baseline in the same aperture and half of the 3.78-day events below.

\textbf{(1) The pre-registered test.} The transit times scatter about a linear ephemeris by 21.8~min rms, against formal uncertainties of 1.7--3.3~min. We find no instrumental cause, and four epochs cannot separate timing systematics from transit-timing variations. On 2026 October~15 we registered the epoch-57 prediction (BJD$_{\rm TDB}$~2461455.9785~$\pm$~30~min), the data to be used, and the criteria that classify the outcome \citep{PreReg}. The test was designed around SPOC two-minute light curves, and its check of the signal's position requires target pixel files.

\textbf{(2) A pipeline mid-time.} The largest contribution to the scatter is the interval between the Sector~100 and 102 transits, which exceeds $P$ by 29~min in our photometry. An independent reduction, the QLP light curves of those sectors, gives $+28.7$~min \citep{Vora2026}, so the excess does not arise in our photometry. A SPOC mid-time at epoch~57 shows whether it persists in the mission pipeline at the next epoch.

\textbf{(3) The 3.78-day signal.} The light curve also contains a $407 \pm 26$~ppm, 1.3-h signal with $P = 3.78371$~d, whose source, the target (as a near-grazing transit) or the $3.5''$ neighbour, the present data cannot identify \citep{Vora2026}. Two-minute cadence would resolve the ingress of the dozen events in the two sectors, which tests the grazing geometry, and the target pixel files would tighten the difference-image centroid, now $5.2'' \pm 6.7''$ from the target.

\section{Target Justification and Feasibility}

We request two-minute cadence for one target, TIC~402710280 (RA~$248.5236^\circ$, Dec~$-63.5497^\circ$), in Sectors~113 and~114, at priority 1.0. \texttt{tess-point} places it on camera~4, CCD~4 (column~385, row~209) in Sector~113 and on camera~4, CCD~1 (column~646, row~475) in Sector~114. The target is fainter than 7th magnitude and needs no enlarged pixel stamp.

The epoch-57 transit is predicted for 2027 February~19 at 11:29~UTC with $\sigma = 30$~min (34~min when the covariance of $T_0$ and $P$ is included), six days after the start of Sector~114 and well inside its first orbit. The neighbouring transits, on 2027 January~14 and March~27, fall outside both sectors.

In FFIs each transit depth is measured to 84--144~ppm, so a single transit is detected at 15--25$\sigma$; SPOC two-minute photometry should match this. Cadence barely changes the timing precision, which is set by photon noise; the gains are SPOC photometry with pipeline systematics correction, target pixel files that allow apertures to be chosen against the $3.5''$ neighbour, and ingress and egress (about 14~min each) sampled by seven cadences instead of four. The ingress shape is what distinguishes a planet from a diluted eclipse of the neighbour.
